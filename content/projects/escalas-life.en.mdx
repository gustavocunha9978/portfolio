## Problem

Building the monthly schedule for sound, worship, media and greeting teams was a puzzle solved by hand on
WhatsApp: people scheduled on days they'd said they couldn't make, the same person in two roles at the same
service, and volunteers who never made it into the rotation.

## Solution

A web app with **separate environments** (each youth ministry with its own visual identity), **role-based
permissions** and every critical rule validated **on the server**, not only in the UI: environment and
ministry membership, compatible role, unavailability and schedule conflicts.

My contributions:

- **Monthly auto-scheduler**: builds a ministry's schedule for every service in the month, respecting
  unavailability and per-person constraints, with rotation scoring and deterministic tie-breaking. The
  preview and the saved schedule are always identical
- Full CRUD and **password recovery**
- **Service order** view for the leadership team
- Security and robustness fixes raised in review

## Result

- A month's schedule generated in seconds, with no conflicts and no unavailable person scheduled
- Fair rotation across volunteers

## Technical challenges

### A predictable auto-scheduler

A randomized generator produces a preview that differs from the saved schedule, and leaders stop trusting
it. Scoring uses explicit weights (rotation, pairs who serve together) and **deterministic tie-breaking**,
covered by tests.

## Lessons learned

- A rule that only exists in the UI isn't a rule.
- An algorithm that decides for people has to be explainable and repeatable.
